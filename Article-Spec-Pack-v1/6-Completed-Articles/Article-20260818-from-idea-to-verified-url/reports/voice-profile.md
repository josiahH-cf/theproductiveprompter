# Iterative Voice Profile

This profile is internal. It turns the article-specific anchor and public reference posts into observable drafting and review criteria.

## Reference Set and Assigned Jobs

### Primary references

- **AI First Principles: How to Decide When AI Is the Right Tool** — Primary cadence and argument reference. Use its direct second-person opening, operational distinctions, compact mental models, skepticism of unnecessary complexity, and action-based close.
- **Day 14: Creative Iteration** — Primary evidence for the voice lesson. Use its concrete near-miss, visible before/after proof, constraint stack, and skeptical critic behavior.
- **Day 30: Documentation That Stays True** — Primary verification reference. Use its short opening beats, drift framing, artifact-first explanation, and insistence that reality—not prose quality—closes the loop.

### Secondary references

- **Day 3: From Logic to Prediction** — Use its familiar opening example, careful concept-building, plain-language caveats, and Before/During/After checklist. Avoid importing its longer textbook-style explanations into this simpler article.
- **Day 31 retrospective** — Use its candid meta perspective, willingness to report failures, and “quality first, automation second” lesson. Do not copy its unsupported industry claims, model rankings, self-reported quality judgments, career predictions, or deliberately provocative tone.

## Stable Voice Traits

1. **Operational before abstract.** Begin with something the reader can observe, inspect, or fail.
2. **Direct without swagger.** Tell the reader what to define or test; do not perform certainty.
3. **Evidence-aware.** Distinguish a documented fact, a project observation, and a working inference.
4. **Constructively skeptical.** Treat autonomy, scores, and green deployment badges as claims that still need proof.
5. **Artifact-led.** Prefer seeds, briefs, drafts, links, rendered pages, and checklists over generic “content” language.
6. **Rhythmic but natural.** Mix short consequence sentences with medium-length explanations; avoid mechanical three-item symmetry in every paragraph.
7. **Quietly candid.** Let incomplete parts of the project remain visible when they teach the mechanism.

## Article-Specific Tone

- Curious about the system's own construction.
- Practical and encouraging enough that a solo blogger can start small.
- More self-auditing than celebratory.
- Technical only when the term changes a decision.
- Mild dry understatement is welcome; jokes and hype are not.

## Recurring Moves to Use Sparingly

- Open a section with a concrete claim or failure.
- Define a pair by consequence: stage/gate, retry/repair, deploy/publish, voice/tone.
- Follow a recommendation with the artifact or check that proves it.
- Use one-sentence paragraphs when the consequence deserves emphasis.
- End with a test or next action.

## Words and Constructions to Watch

- Avoid “unlock,” “leverage,” “transform,” “game-changing,” “seamless,” and “powerful” unless technically necessary or quoted.
- Avoid repeated “It is not X. It is Y.” framing. Demonstrate the difference with an artifact or outcome.
- Avoid “simply,” “just,” and “obviously” when they dismiss real implementation work.
- Avoid calling an LLM a writer, editor, or judge without clarifying the bounded role it performs.
- Avoid treating “your voice” as a mystical essence; describe the examples, choices, and corrections that make it observable.

## Voice Calibration Loop to Teach

1. Select a small, diverse reference set and assign each sample a job.
2. Extract tentative traits, recurring moves, topic-dependent tones, and anti-patterns.
3. Generate two or three short alternatives for a real passage.
4. Ask the author to rank them and explain the near miss in concrete terms.
5. Turn the correction into a profile rule and a regression example.
6. Test the updated profile on a different article type.
7. Keep the change only if it improves voice fit without reducing meaning preservation or naturalness.

## Three-Part Voice Gate

### Voice fit

- Does the passage make the kinds of choices the reference set demonstrates?
- Are critiques tied to words, cadence, structure, or rhetorical moves rather than an unexplained score?
- Does the situational tone fit this article without changing the stable voice?

### Meaning preservation

- Are factual claims, uncertainty, citations, commitments, and the author's position unchanged?
- Did the rewrite add lived experience, opinions, or certainty the source did not contain?
- Are links, quotations, code, and required formatting intact?

### Naturalness

- Does the passage read as deliberate prose rather than a rubric rendered into sentences?
- Does sentence length vary without becoming choppy?
- Have repeated contrasts, canned transitions, inflated summaries, and symmetrical list rhythms been removed?

Any failed dimension returns the passage to a targeted repair. Disagreement between an automated critic and the author's ranking escalates to the author.

## Held-Out Regression Cases

Use these after drafting and after the final naturalization pass:

1. **Explain idempotent publication without using the word “idempotent.”** Passing text tells the reader to check whether the first publish happened before repeating it and names the duplicate-side-effect risk.
2. **Describe the project's client-rendered article route.** Passing text says the raw response is a generic shell and the browser later fetches Markdown; it does not claim Google cannot index the page.
3. **Explain voice research.** Passing text says examples help but are insufficient under the cited study's setup, names the study's lack of large-scale human evaluation, and labels the proposed loop as a synthesis.
4. **Describe the current launcher.** Passing text credits discovery and control loading while stating that dynamic model routing remains unimplemented.
5. **Close the article.** Passing text gives one small real-world trial and tells the reader what evidence to record; it does not end in a maxim.

## Pairwise Review Rule

When comparing two revisions, evaluate them twice with order reversed. Require passage-level reasons before accepting a preference. Treat inconsistent rankings as weak evidence and send consequential disagreements to the author.
