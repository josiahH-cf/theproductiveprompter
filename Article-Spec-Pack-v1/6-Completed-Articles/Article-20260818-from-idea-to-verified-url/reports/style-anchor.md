# Article-Specific Style Anchor

## Anchor Passage

Start with the outcome you can inspect. A generated draft is not a published article, just as a green deployment is not proof that the right page reached a reader. Define the work in stages. Let each stage leave behind something concrete: a seed, an approved intent, a sourced draft, a package, a live URL. Put a decision between stages whenever failure should change the path. If the evidence is weak, return to research. If the voice feels borrowed, return to calibration. If a publish request times out, check what happened before trying again. Keep the machinery visible enough to trust and simple enough to use. Then run one real article through it and improve the first place it breaks.

## Calibration Notes

- Open from a concrete operating failure or observable outcome.
- Prefer short claims followed by one explanatory paragraph or example.
- Use direct second-person language without sounding like a tutorial template.
- Translate technical machinery into artifacts, decisions, and consequences.
- Let the meta case study carry the personality; do not turn it into a victory lap.
- Use restrained dry phrasing where it clarifies a gap: “That solved scheduling. It did not solve publication.”
- End sections by telling the reader what to check, change, or try next.

## Anti-Patterns

- Generic AI enthusiasm, marketing language, and inflated claims.
- Repeated “not X, but Y” constructions without a concrete demonstration.
- Dense taxonomies, implementation syntax, or framework tours.
- Voice described only with adjectives.
- Symmetrical list-writing that makes every section sound generated.
- First-person claims that cannot be supported by the public project record.
- Metaphors that outlive their explanatory value.
