Dot as Her, or what Siri was supposed to become

By Josiah Hunter•October 20, 2026•3 min read

A phone-call handoff while using my dot failed, and I do not know why. It mattered because continuity had already become the useful part of the experience. I had begun to expect a conversation to carry into the next piece of work without requiring me to rebuild all the relevant context. When the handoff failed, I was reminded that my expectation had moved ahead of what the experience could reliably give me.

OpenAI calls its persistent assistants in ChatGPT dots; an individual assistant is a dot. The idea is that you give an assistant a goal and enough context to keep working between conversations, using its own computer and apps you connect. OpenAI's getting-started guide explains the concept; its sections on memory and controls explain how context and permissions fit into it. What interests me is how that feels when I am trying to get something done.

Before this, I was already working toward something similar with Obsidian Agent, or OA. It connected the goals, projects and context in my Obsidian notes to work that an assistant could carry out and check. It was my attempt to bring personal context and practical follow-through together, with a way to capture requests and coordinate the tools doing the work.

So the change is more specific than going from isolated chats to an assistant that remembers things. With my dot, much of the conversation, gathering of context and coordination I was building around OA now happens through the assistant I am talking to. That supersedes a substantial part of OA's role for me. OA still supports local execution and some of the controls around it; it has not simply disappeared. But I can spend more of the interaction explaining what I want to accomplish.

I have felt that difference in day planning and flight research. The value is not some dramatic new capability in either category. It is that a conversation can belong to a larger thread of work instead of standing alone. When relevant context carries forward while the immediate subject changes, I spend less time reconstructing where I was and can focus more on the decision or task in front of me.

I notice it most in coding coordination. The conversation and the coding work that follows can feel connected, rather than like two separate starts. I still need to check what actually happened. Remembering a request, passing it along and completing it are different things, and a smooth conversation does not make those distinctions go away. The useful part is being able to stay with the work when enough of the context survives.

That is the limited sense in which Her comes to mind. The film is a metaphor for a conversational presence that maintains continuity over time, not a claim about consciousness or feelings. “What Siri was supposed to become” is personal language, too. It describes what I once expected a digital assistant to become, not a promise Apple made: something that could remain present across different kinds of work instead of waiting inside a single interaction.

The phone-call failure belongs in that picture. Once I had begun to expect the thread to continue, a break in it felt larger than a conversation simply ending. I do not have enough evidence to explain that particular failure. It would be easy to supply a plausible technical reason, but that would add certainty I do not have.

My experience with my dot is useful and unfinished at the same time. It takes over much of the assistant role I was trying to assemble with OA, while still depending on separate tools and work that need checking. The appeal is practical: less time rebuilding context, more chance of carrying an intention through to a result. I do not need it to seem human. I need the next handoff to work.
