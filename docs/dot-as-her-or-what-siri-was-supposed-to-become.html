Dot as Her, or what Siri was supposed to become

By Josiah Hunter•October 20, 2026•3 min read

A phone-call handoff with Dot failed, and I do not know why. It mattered because continuity had already become the useful part of the experience. I had begun to expect a conversation to carry into the next piece of work without requiring me to rebuild all the relevant context. When the handoff failed, I was reminded that my expectation had moved ahead of what the experience could reliably give me.

Before Dot, I often began a new assistant conversation by rebuilding the relevant context. I had to decide what background to repeat, which earlier decisions mattered, and how much explanation the current request needed. Dot changes that for me when selected context carries forward. I do not treat that continuity as a complete record of everything I have said. It is partial, but it can still be useful.

I have felt that difference in day planning and flight research. The value is not some dramatic new capability in either category. It is that a conversation can belong to a larger thread of work instead of standing alone. When relevant context carries forward while the immediate subject changes, I spend less time reconstructing where I was and can focus more on the decision or task in front of me.

I notice the value of selected continuity most in coding coordination. A conversation and the coding work that follows can feel connected, rather than like two separate starts, when Dot carries some of the relevant context forward. That connection is never something I take for granted: I do not expect every detail to follow me or every handoff between tools to work cleanly. But when it does hold, I can stay with the work instead of spending the first part of it reconstructing where I was.

The useful moments and the frustrating ones belong to the same story. Carrying a little context forward can make an ordinary task easier to pick up again. It can also raise my expectations for the next handoff. That is the part I keep coming back to: once a conversation starts to feel connected to the work, a break in that connection is harder to shrug off.

That is the limited sense in which Her comes to mind. I am not comparing Dot to a conscious or emotional character. The film is simply a useful metaphor for a conversational presence that maintains continuity over time. “What Siri was supposed to become” is personal language, too. It describes what I once expected a digital assistant to become, not a promise Apple made: something that could remain present across different kinds of work instead of waiting inside a single interaction.

The comparison only holds for me when the continuity holds, which is why the phone-call failure matters. Because I had come to expect the thread to continue, its break felt larger than a conversation simply ending. I do not have enough evidence to explain that particular failure, and I do not need to invent an explanation for it to recognize the gap it left.

My experience with Dot is useful and unfinished at the same time. Day planning, flight research, and coding coordination have shown me why selected continuity can matter. The failed phone-call handoff has shown me how quickly that value weakens when the thread does not carry through. Dot is valuable to me because it can preserve enough context to help real work continue across conversations and tools. That value is practical and does not depend on Dot seeming human. For now, the useful continuity is real in my experience, and so is the gap left by that failed handoff.
