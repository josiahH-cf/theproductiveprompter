Dot as Her, or what Siri was supposed to become

By Josiah Hunter•October 20, 2026•5 min read

I started a call with my dot, and a human-sounding voice walked me through what I needed to do.

In my time using it, I’ve booked flights, arranged grocery deliveries, riffed on ideas, and built substantial features. I’ve used it to work through goals and outcomes, consider whether an idea is feasible, and ask what would prove it wrong. There is something strange about doing all of that through conversation.

I know I’m talking to software. I’m also starting to feel as though the models have personalities and real understanding. Knowing about context, parameters, and probabilistic generation hasn’t stopped that feeling.

OpenAI calls its persistent assistants in ChatGPT dots. The part I want to explore is what happens when using one starts to feel like talking with someone who understands what I’m trying to do.

Why it starts to feel personal

Travel planning, groceries, software, and an unfinished idea ask for different kinds of attention. Being able to work through them with the same conversational interface changes how the interaction feels. I can talk about something I want to accomplish, explore whether it makes sense, and work toward a result.

I’ve used these conversations to test feasibility and falsifiability: could this work, and what evidence would show that it doesn’t? When an exchange helps me examine an idea that way, it can feel as though the model understands what I’m trying to get at.

That is where Her comes to mind. The comparison is about a conversational presence that can remain familiar while the subject changes. It captures the feeling of the interaction; it leaves the question of an inner life open.

“What Siri was supposed to become” describes my own expectation of a digital assistant: something I could talk to while moving through different parts of my life. A flight, a grocery order, a feature, an idea. The conversation could stay with the work.

I’ve written about how context, tools, and feedback turn a request into work. Using a dot gives that subject a more personal dimension. The context that helps it follow the task also helps me experience the conversation as something continuous.

Would I be able to tell?

In his 1950 paper, *Computing Machinery and Intelligence*, Alan Turing proposed the imitation game as a way to make the question of machine thinking more concrete. His setup used written exchanges, deliberately excluding the sound of a voice. The test concerned what an interrogator could distinguish from the answers. It did not establish that a convincing exchange proves consciousness or sentience.

My experience is different. I already know I’m speaking to AI, and the voice contributes to the experience. Even so, if I had to judge from the conversation alone, I’m not sure I could always tell the difference.

That makes me ask what we mean by artificial general intelligence. I’m using the assistant across several kinds of work, including thinking through what to build and how to test it. What would I need to observe before I called that general intelligence? How much of my impression comes from the work it can do, and how much comes from the way it talks with me?

Sounding human, working competently across different tasks, and having a subjective experience are separate questions. A feature can be tested. A booking can be checked. Those results give me something concrete to evaluate. They don’t settle what, if anything, the system experiences.

I can describe what the assistant helped me do and how the conversation felt. The harder question is how much I can conclude from that feeling.

What is happening underneath

OpenAI’s getting-started guide describes an assistant that can carry work across conversations, using its own computer and connected apps. The technical details help me make sense of the experience while keeping track of what I’m inferring from it.

The model and the context do different jobs. Training adjusts a model’s numerical parameters. When it generates text, those learned parameters help determine probabilities over tokens, which can be words, parts of words, or punctuation. The information supplied for the current response shapes what it generates. OpenAI explains the distinction in its overview of how its models are developed.

Context can include the conversation, instructions, and information supplied for the task. The product can also carry information forward: OpenAI says a dot can receive ChatGPT memories and save context of its own. Each task still has its own conversation and does not automatically receive every conversation I’ve had with the dot. Remembered information being available to the product is different from all of it being present in every response.

The voice and the work can involve several components. OpenAI’s voice-agent documentation describes several arrangements, including direct speech-to-speech models and systems that connect a voice interface to a separate reasoning and tool workflow. That documentation explains possible designs; it does not identify the exact arrangement behind my call.

Tools and connected services are what let a conversational assistant act on a task. Remembering a request, passing it along and completing it are different things. A completed booking or a feature’s test results provide evidence beyond the spoken reply.

The settings shape what context and access are available. OpenAI’s memory and controls documentation and dots privacy FAQ explain the distinctions:

Setting What it changes
MemoryChatGPT memory can be shared with the dot, which also retains its own context. Turning off shared memory does not erase information the dot already received.
PluginsConnected apps supply information and supported actions within the permissions granted to them.
Custom rulesRules govern which supported actions can proceed independently, need explicit authorization or confirmation, or require your intervention. Built-in safeguards still apply.
Improve the model for everyoneFor personal accounts, this setting controls whether dot conversations and work may be used to improve OpenAI’s models. It is separate from what the dot remembers for ongoing work.

On mobile, the dot’s Customize menu contains Memory, Plugins, and Custom rules. The Memory screen shows shared ChatGPT memory settings, rather than a complete editable record of the dot’s own retained context. OpenAI currently says individual dot memories cannot be viewed or edited directly.

Those components give me a way to understand how useful continuity is built. They don’t remove the feeling that started this article. I called an assistant, worked through things I needed to do, and found myself wondering how I would tell the difference between a system that understands me and one that can respond as though it does.
