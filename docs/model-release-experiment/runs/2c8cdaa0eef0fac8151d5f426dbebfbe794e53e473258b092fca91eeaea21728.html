1. THE STORY

The White Margin

At two o’clock, Mara set four order cards beneath the paperweight shaped like a pear: posters, tickets, menus, theater banners. Outside, the shop’s awning snapped in the wind. Inside, the printer smelled of hot dust even when it wasn’t running.

Ivo lifted the banner card.

“Those first?”

“Those included,” Mara said. “Different promise.”

The theater had lent the shop its folding chairs for every neighborhood meeting. Ivo had painted scenery there since he was sixteen. His sleeve still carried a streak of blue from a sea that would appear onstage tomorrow.

Mara tapped her pencil against the cards. Eighty dollars of materials, exactly. One hundred twenty machine minutes, exactly. Payments of $294, less the materials, left $214. After the afternoon’s fixed $35 operating expense, the repair fund would gain $179.

Forty-one dollars short.

Ivo looked at the figures upside down. “That’s close.”

“It’s forty-one dollars.”

“You make close sound like a disease.”

Mara turned the banner card toward him. “I put your sea in.”

He smiled then, despite himself.

Before they opened a packet of paper, Mara counted the materials envelope. She counted it twice, flattening each bill against the counter with the side of her hand.

Sixty-six dollars.

The eighty on her worksheet was wrong. The envelope was right.

She drew a line through the four cards’ total.

Ivo stopped smiling. “We can do the banners and decide the rest afterward.”

“No. Payments come after production ends. We have to buy everything with what’s here.”

“Then do fewer other things.”

“That’s what I’m checking.”

He leaned against the counter while she worked. Beyond the glass door, someone had left a bicycle chained to the empty newspaper rack. Its loose wheel clicked whenever the wind caught it.

Mara moved the cards into rows, penciling totals on the backs. Twice she reached for the banner card, then set it down again.

Finally she placed posters, programs, tickets, and bookmarks beneath the pear.

“Sixty-six in materials. A hundred minutes. Two hundred forty-five paid afterward.”

Ivo stared at the small card marked BOOKMARKS.

“You’re dropping the theater for those?”

“The programs are for the theater.”

“You know what I mean.”

She did. The banners would hang across the entrance, bright enough to make people stop. Bookmarks would disappear into books. Nobody would photograph a bookmark and thank the print shop.

“These leave a hundred seventy-nine before the operating expense,” she said. “A hundred forty-four for the repair.”

“And with banners?”

“Banners, programs, tickets: a hundred forty-one for the repair.”

“Three dollars.”

“Yes.”

“For three dollars, you’d tell them no?”

Mara’s pencil had worn a gray crescent into her thumbnail. She looked at it instead of him.

“For three dollars, I’d have to tell somebody else no. I’d still be short. I’m trying to be short by as little as I can.”

Ivo rubbed the blue paint on his sleeve. It had dried too hard to move.

“Someone said a donor might give a hundred tonight.”

“Someone might.”

“You don’t have to sound like that.”

She put the pencil down.

“I want them to give it,” she said. “I want to stop counting. But I can’t put a might in this envelope.”

For a moment neither touched the cards.

Then Ivo took out his phone and called the theater. He explained the programs would be ready, but the banners wouldn’t. He listened longer than he spoke.

When he hung up, he asked, “Where’s the bookmark file?”

Mara pointed to the computer.

They purchased the materials. The printer began its hundred minutes of work, pulling sheets with a steady rubber whisper. Ivo caught the programs as they emerged. Mara trimmed posters. Neither mentioned the sea.

The bookmarks carried a drawing of the neighborhood’s old elm. Ivo noticed that one branch ran beyond the cutting guide.

“That’ll get chopped.”

Mara came to look. Their shoulders touched.

“Move it inward a little,” she said.

He did.

After production ended, they collected $245. Mara set aside $66 to restore the materials float, paid the $35 operating expense, and counted $144 toward the repair.

The gap was $76. Ivo checked his phone. There was no gift, no commitment.

He helped Mara tape that number inside the window. Then he held the door while she turned out the lights.
