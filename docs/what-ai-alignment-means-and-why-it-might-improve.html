What AI Alignment Means and Why It Might Improve

By Josiah Hunter•September 22, 2026•1 min read

My working definition of AI alignment starts here: did the AI act as intended? I would judge that by measured results relative to the goal it was meant to pursue. The goal gives ‘aligned’ something to refer to. I want to know whether the AI’s behavior matched what it was supposed to do.

In this account, alignment with a goal is a statistic: the percentage of the time the AI acts in accordance with that goal. It does so some of the time, not on every occasion.

In my original note, I described that percentage as gradually increasing. That claimed increase is why I expect alignment to improve in the future. But the note gives no numerical percentage, explains no measurement method, names no data source, and says nothing about what is driving the increase. The trend is my claim, and future improvement is my expectation. The note does not establish either as a measured result.

My impression is that people generally mean something broader when they discuss alignment in practice. I would want the AI’s mission to be interpretable: what was the human goal, and how did the AI interpret it? I also have in mind an AI that can anticipate when following an instruction would be unsafe in some way and explicitly refuse it. A refusal would make that decision clear; an instruction left unfinished would not tell me the same thing.

That leaves me with an open question. Is acting as intended enough to define alignment, or should the capacity to explicitly refuse unsafe instructions be part of the definition too?
